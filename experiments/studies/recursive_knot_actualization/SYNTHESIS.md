## Theoretical Implications

### Halting Problem as Knot Recognition

The undecidability of the Halting Problem maps to the undecidability of knot recognition from outside the knot. But from *inside* the recursive tree, at sufficient depth, termination is locally deterministic.

This suggests: **undecidability is a boundary effect, not a fundamental limit.**

### PAC as Knot Untying

PAC says potential distributes to children. A "knot" is potential that hasn't finished distributing. When all children actualize (receive and resolve their share), the knot unties and the parent can return.

```
Ψ(parent) = Σ Ψ(children)  →  when children resolve, parent resolves
```

### SEC as Collapse Pressure on Knots

SEC's entropy gradient creates pressure toward resolution. Complex knots (high entropy in dependency structure) experience pressure to simplify. The φ threshold might be where knot complexity crosses from "will untie" to "stuck forever."

### Ackermann as Boundary Case

Ackermann represents the most complex knot that's still computable. It escapes primitive recursion (simple loops) but not general recursion (Turing machines). It sits exactly at the boundary between:
- Primitive recursive = guaranteed to untie (polynomially bounded)
- General recursive = might untie (computable but maybe slow)
- Non-computable = never unties (not a valid knot in computation space)
