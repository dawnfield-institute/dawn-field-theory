## What the arms actually did

**Where the 2.8 σ went.** The κ = 1 arm reproduced: its mean is 0.74 on seeds 4–6 against 0.75 on
seeds 1–3. What moved is the control. Gravity alone holds **0.645 / 0.631 / 0.684** on the fresh
seeds — high and tight, σ 0.027 — where on seeds 1–3 it held **0.395 / 0.315 / 0.608**, low and
spread, σ 0.151. The exp_29 margin was gravity's two low seeds. Over the six seeds gravity's own
window-mean percolation ranges 0.32–0.68; the ledgered arms at κ ≤ 1 range 0.44–0.85. The claim
"κ = 1 holds more than gravity by 2 σ" needed gravity to be reliably low, and it is not.

**The virial arithmetic predicted an arm it had never seen.** κ = 1.5 was registered as *predicted*
— between κ = 1 and κ = 2 on every KE/|U| measure, holding less web than κ = 1 — and that is
where it landed in every seed: KE/|U| 0.43 (κ = 1: 0.33–0.36; exp_29's κ = 2: 0.58–0.59), bound at
every mark, percolation 0.19–0.39. The ordering at full size on fresh seeds is
**0.5 ≈ 1 > 0 > 1.5 > ∞**; exp_29's was 1 > 0.5 > 0 > 2 > ∞.

**The edge is where the pressure's net work changes sign.** At κ = 0.5 the bounded pressure does
net negative work (−4.0 / −4.3 / −4.1 P₀), at κ = 1 still negative (−1.4 / −1.1 / −0.8 P₀), and the
web stands. At κ = 1.5 it is **positive** (+0.23 / +0.21 / +0.20 P₀; exp_29's κ = 2: +0.28 to +0.30)
and the web is gone — below gravity alone, with occupancy up (0.18 vs 0.09 for gravity) and the
Ξ-anchored structure statistic down (ξ_u 0.74–0.77 against 1.27–1.75 at κ ≤ 1). The ledger makes
this edge visible as a number: between one and one-and-a-half binding energies of budget the
repulsion stops being paid for by collapse and starts being paid for by the budget, and the web
does not survive that. This is a description of the sweep, not a registered claim.

**Exploring, unscored — and the reason not to chase it.** On the fresh seeds the arm that looks best
against gravity is now κ = 0.5 (0.796 / 0.700 / 0.782; 3/3; margin 0.106, pooled σ 0.041, 2.6 σ)
— post hoc, at a different budget from the one registered, exactly as exp_29's κ = 1 was post hoc
at a different budget from *its* registered κ = 0.5. Pooling all six seeds: κ = 0.5 sits above
gravity 6/6 at **1.0 σ**, κ = 1 5/6 at **1.6 σ**; 11/12 seed–arm pairs in the same direction, and
neither arm near 2 σ. The direction is consistent and the effect, if it exists, is small against
seed variance. The best-looking arm moving from κ = 1 to κ = 0.5 between seed sets is what seed
noise looks like when the arm is chosen after the result. A six-seed registration at one budget
could resolve it; it is not what Milestone R needs and is not registered here.

## The reading

exp_29 ended with two findings and one number. The findings — the ledger makes the substrate bound
and conserving, and the web survives the bounded engine by 4.8–5.7 σ — reproduce here at every seed
(κ = ∞ at 0.023–0.027 against 0.57–0.85). The number — κ = 1 beats gravity alone by 2.8 σ — was
the control's seeds, and the registered claim built on it is dead by its own kill sentence.

What stands is narrower and sharper than the claim that died. The ledgered engine is a
*bound-maker*: at κ = 1 it settles at KE/|U| ≈ ⅓, the minimum of the U-shape, with the pressure
paying for itself. It is not, at any budget tested and to the resolution three seeds give, a
*structure-adder* beyond gravity. And it has an edge: one budget step above κ = 1 the pressure's
net work turns positive and the web goes. R2 — severance on a bound substrate — has what it needs
and did not have before exp_29: a substrate that holds a web without a speed cap or a tuned drag,
at a declared budget, with a conserved total. It runs at κ = 1 as the kill sentence says; κ = 0.5
is the conservative choice if R2 wants margin below the edge (bound at KE/|U| 0.53, the pressure
still doing −4 P₀ of work), and that is Peter's call at R2's registration, not a change here.

**Exploring ≠ predicting:** every number in "the edge" and "the reason not to chase it" is
description of a sweep that was registered for something else.
