### 1. Introduction

Conventional approaches to entropy treat it as a measure of disorder or information loss. However, in symbolic systems, field theory, and cognitive simulations, entropy may also mask unresolved structure. This paper proposes a technique for separating structured symbolic components from truly disordered entropy using recursive filtering and entropy quantification.

We define this approach as **Recursive Entropy Decomposition (RED)**—a symbolic diagnostic protocol rooted in the principles of Dawn Field Theory.

### 2. Motivation

Understanding and reducing entropy is central to:

The need is growing for methods that not only measure entropy but can *diagnose its structure and origin*. This work begins addressing that need.

### 3. Methodology

We simulate layered signals composed of:

* Base symbolic structures (low frequency)
* Mid-layer oscillations (medium frequency)
* High-frequency turbulence (entropy proxies)
* True noise (random, structureless entropy)

Using bandpass filters, we extract each symbolic band and compute the residual, classifying entropy into:

* `E_signal`: valid, interpretable structure
* `E_overlap`: mixed symbolic layers
* `E_noise`: unresolved entropy

Entropy is measured using normalized Shannon entropy. All experiments are initially run using Lorenz attractor waveforms as test signals.

---

### 4. Results (Preliminary)

Initial simulations show:

* Entropy separation is effective with well-formed chaotic signals
* Residuals preserve non-obvious structure
* `E_noise` isolation may enable further recursive modeling

These results support the hypothesis that what appears entropic may be layer-mixed structure.
