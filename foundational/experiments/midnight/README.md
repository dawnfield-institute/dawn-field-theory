**Tractability:** Near-term. Archival spectroscopy data exists. The prediction (constant alpha, epoch-dependent line width) is testable against existing quasar absorption line databases.

**Dependencies:** M1 (alpha derivation), M6 (Fibonacci depth coupling), M8 (alpha at 5.7 ppm), M-R (line width / disequilibrium, coupling energy scale).

### Thread 5: The Phase-Rate Primitive

**Question:** Is there a single field — the local phase-rate `w(x)` — from which both relativity and decoherence emerge as distinct properties? And does it predict new, unnamed effects?

**The primitive:** `w = (mc²/hbar) * (dtau/dt)` — the rate an internal quantum clock advances at a given embedding in the connection structure. Relativity is how `w` is *shifted* (mean sector: reversible, needs no environment). Decoherence is how `w` is *spread* (variance sector: irreversible, requires an environment). Two properties of one field, not two phenomena.

**Discriminating test — the isolated fast atom:** A single atom in vacuum moving near c time-dilates (mean of `w` shifts) but does not decohere (zero variance — no environment). A still atom in a thermal bath decoheres (large variance in `w`) but does not dilate (mean unchanged). The two effects separate cleanly along the first two moments of `w`, as expected if they are distinct properties of one quantity rather than the same property twice.

**The prediction that matters — gravity gates entanglement:** If entanglement formation requires commensurate phase-rates (harmonic locking of `w`), and gravitational embedding sets `w` via `g_out = g_in²`, then entanglement-formation probability across a gravitational gradient should show structure — peaks at commensurate phase-rate ratios — not a flat background. Standard QM predicts flat. DFT predicts peaks at phi^n ratios. A measurement can tell them apart.

**The deeper consequence:** If `w` is genuinely primitive (not just a statistical summary), there is no reason relativity and decoherence are its *only* properties. Higher-order moments may correspond to effects not yet named. The primitive-with-properties view can *generate*; the two-statistics view can only unify what's already known.

**Connection to framework:** `w` appears to be the depth-weighted SEC clock rate: `w(x) = phi^(-d) * c² / hbar * (dtau/dt)`, where `d` is Fibonacci depth and `dtau/dt` comes from SEC complexification. The mean sector maps to M12's connection structure. The variance sector maps to M14's measurement interaction (gauge fixing distributes phase across orbits).

**Tractability:** Near-term for formalization. The gravity-gates-entanglement prediction is experimentally testable with current technology (atomic clocks already detect gravitational redshift at centimeter scale).

**Dependencies:** M12 (connection as primitive, `g_out = g_in²`), M13 (identity as complement, Weyl group), M14 (measurement as gauge fixing, entanglement from product graphs).
